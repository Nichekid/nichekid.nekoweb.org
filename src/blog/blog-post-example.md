People aren't Caricatures.

People aren't Cartoons.

I mean you and I probably already knew that but, what most people who aren't familiar with drawing don't know, (or aren't conciously aware), is that caricatures and cartoons, (so to speak,) are merely simplifications of people. Sometimes they highlight our "pretty" parts and sometimes they highlight our "ugly" parts. 

Because of this, drawn people tend to be more "perfect" than real people, with perfect parts, like perfect hair, eyes, waist, face and more. Sometimes they tend to highlight, our so called "worse" features, like our wrinkles or overly buck teeth, our long nose. Some drawings are **(conventionally)** pretty, and some drawings are grotesque. 

But whats to say about our views on them? Do we shun ourselves when we see our split teeth so usually mocked on tv? Do we wish we had the body of the pretty lady on that comic book you read? Perhaps we have all at some point wished we had to have the body of that cool animated character we liked, just cause we liked them y'know? They are cool and do cool things! Sometimes though, it can be a little deeper than that. Most of the time, its because we are unsatisfied with our own bodies.

<img src="/images/blog/real-people-caricatures-of-people/RPCOP-1.webp" width="30%">

We use those characters as our points of beauty, or better said, standards of beauty in which we base ourselves on. but saying it truthfully, its all in vain. While yes, animated people tend to be based off of, y'know, REAL people, its not fair to compare ourselves to a caricature.

When you learn how to draw, your usually given a base on how to start to draw people. Usually a broad shoulder male body, and a curvy female body. Not to say we don't naturally have those shapes, but, sometimes they are simplified to the point where their individuality is taken away. 

That beauty mark on their left check? Gone. The slightly chipped tooth they had from playing with loved ones? Erased. Their eye wrinkles collected from many days of hardwork? (And lack of sleep?) Cleaned up. Those stretch marks on their hips showing how their weight has changed over their life? Like it was never there. That grey hair tricking up from your ears like marks of a soul that has lived through quite a few years? Swept away like the wind.

**(Of course their is something to be said about the fact that they are the default due to it being easier for beginners to learn and capture basic shapes! This is not what I'm saying.)**

But so and so you say, "Those are quite unglamourous things about humans! It's nice that we can at least enjoy the purely "good things" about us in fiction!" I don't think theres anything **inheriently**. wrong with drawing a cute girl or a pretty boy on your screen, but I think that, if the ONLY thing we like about our species are the "good parts", then in essence, we love the caricature. Not the human. Not us.

Because frankly, most of us aren't perfect lookin', even those who seem that way on the outside! real humans, we have imperfections. We have things that show our wear and tear, we have things that show that we are awake and alive. That we have gone through things. Big and small, serious and not serious things that completely changed the trijectory of our life, and things that haven't mattered at all. 

<img src="/images/blog/real-people-caricatures-of-people/RPCOP-2.webp" width="30%">

Why don't we try to capture the true beauty of humans in our works? And remind people that they are beautiful in many ways!

You may try to hide your imperfections, but in reality you are hiding who you truly are and your legacy as a human and as a soul. Because, in reality, everything that makes up you, is beautiful.

Anyways, what's not to love? I mean, we are our own us! you are your own you. There is not one person out there that is like you, and has exactly what you have. Every single person person on the planet earth is thier own self and are all unique. 

We come in all sorts of shapes and sizes! Sometimes our heads are kind alike squares, sometimes they are like triangles, and sometimes they are as round as the rim of the sun that shines so brightly above. Sometimes we have all the melanin, and soemtimes we have very little of it! Sometimes our hair can be as long as Rapunzel herself, and sometimes our heads are as shiny as a diamond in the rough. Sometimes we've got big ol' eyes, sometimes we have long noses, sometimes we have big perky lips, or very very thin lips.

I could try to list all the variations, but it'll take me the whole book to do so! In essence, I feel that, since we all come in so many wonderful shapes and size, we should show that more! So that slowly, we start to love, not the caricature, but ourselves. 

...I guess thats easier said than done though. Can't expect everyone to agree with you. And as the individuals we are, many may have different opinions on this! 

Whatever those opinions may be though, at least we can always start with ourselves. We can take a step back and ask ourselves, truly, do we love our fellow humans? OR do we love the caricature of perfection we are usually protrayed as?
